## 3. What's genuinely strong

- **The bot-protection build is careful, not just checked off** — the admin dev-tool exemption bug (an early version would have silently failed to exempt the common case) was caught and fixed properly by splitting into a public/internal function pair that a browser has no code path to reach, not a boolean flag that could be spoofed.
- **Rate-limit design fails open, deliberately** — an infra hiccup degrades to "no rate limiting today," never to "bookings are broken." That's the right failure direction for a feature whose entire job is not blocking real customers.
- **Patient self-service cancel is a complete, well-thought-out flow** — window-based gating, request-vs-outright-cancel distinction, clinic notification, duplicate-request guard. This is retention-relevant work done right, not rushed.
- **The holidays feature, despite being built at demo speed, still went through Arun's live-test-and-fix loop** (two rounds of "Arun tested live" fixes already landed — inline edit, UI capitalization, subtitle removal) rather than shipping untouched.

## 4. Gaps that block revenue or retention — ranked

1. **Confirm the rate-limit migration actually ran, and get the holidays feature live-tested, before/during the demo.** Both are "code done, live-DB/testing status unconfirmed" as of their last workflow update, and holidays specifically is about to be shown to a prospective client days after being written. A booking-conflict bug or a broken availability calculation in front of a paying prospect costs more than the week it would take to verify this properly. *Why it matters for €1M ARR: a bad first demo of a feature built specifically to close a sale defeats its own purpose.*
2. **Legal pages are still placeholder text, three reviews running.** The engineering blocker is gone (shell's done) — what's left is entirely on the external legal review Arun needs to commission. This has been the single most repeated finding across all three reviews and the actual remaining work isn't code. *Why it matters: this is health-adjacent patient data under GDPR; a real clinic's compliance officer (or a savvy patient) could notice a live "document is in preparation" privacy policy.*
3. **No owner-facing analytics/reporting, unmoved for three reviews.** A clinic owner today has no way to see their own booking volume, no-show rate, or bono usage trends without you building it for them ad hoc. *Why it matters: this is a stickiness gap — an owner who can't see the platform is actually helping them has one less reason to keep paying once the novelty wears off.*
4. **WhatsApp booking is sandbox-only for any real clinic**, including the newly-added manual-booking confirmations and reminders — going live requires each clinic to complete their own Meta Business verification and get message templates pre-approved. This is documented in the workflow file but is a real gap between what the feature can do in a demo and what it can do for a self-serve signup on day one. *Why it matters: if WhatsApp booking is being pitched as a live differentiator, the actual onboarding friction for a real clinic to use it needs to be set correctly, not discovered post-sale.*

## 5. Blind spots not currently tracked

- **Nothing tracks multi-location/multi-provider scaling** beyond the brand-new (and still per-clinic, not per-location) holidays work — worth a note now that a second per-provider dimension exists, since the two together start to matter for a clinic with more than a couple of practitioners.
- **Mobile experience has no dedicated tracking anywhere** in `workflows/` — for a product whose buyer persona is a solo practitioner likely checking bookings between patients on a phone, this is worth at least a deliberate "we checked, it's fine" note rather than silence.
- **The rate-limit migration ambiguity itself is a process blind spot**, not just a one-off gap: a workflow step can sit at "pending live migration" indefinitely with nothing forcing a resolution. Worth a lighter-weight nudge (even just re-reading `in_progress` steps with a "pending migration" note at the top of each review) so this class of finding doesn't require a fresh review to resurface.

## 6. Technical risk with business consequences

- **The holidays/time-off feature is the largest block of unexercised production code in the repo right now** — six interlocking `in_progress` steps (recurring holidays, provider time-off, manual-booking closure warnings, availability-engine integration, existing-bookings conflict alerts, a conflicts tab), most still pending their own live-DB migration (`schema_subset_016.sql`), built in two days specifically to support an imminent demo. If the availability-engine integration has an edge case, the failure mode is a real slot showing as bookable when it shouldn't be (or vice versa) — directly patient-facing.
- **Silent-failure-by-design is now the pattern across three separate systems** (WhatsApp sends, bono trigger, rate-limiting) with only `console.error`/Vercel logs as the safety net, and no one reading them systematically. Each one individually is a reasonable degrade-gracefully choice; together, with Sentry still untouched, it means the platform has no way to notice a slow accumulation of failures across all three at once.
- Everything from the payments/bonos side (Stripe billing, session-package deduction) remains as solid as last review — no new risk there.

## 7. My recommended next 3

1. **Before/immediately after the demo: confirm the two pending live migrations ran (`schema_subset_008` rate-limiting, `schema_subset_016` holidays) and actually exercise the holidays flow against real data.** This is the single highest-leverage thing to close right now — it protects both a carried-over go-live blocker and brand-new demo-critical code with one pass.
2. **Get the GDPR legal review moving independently of any engineering work** — it's the only remaining piece of the longest-standing recommendation in this review's history, and the lead time is external, not something more engineering time fixes.
3. **Ship the cheap interim error-visibility option from `error-monitoring.md`** (a scheduled digest of `[client-error]`/`[whatsapp]`/`[rate-limit]` logs, not necessarily full Sentry) — with three separate silent-fail-by-design systems now live, "someone happens to check Vercel logs" is no longer a reasonable safety net.
